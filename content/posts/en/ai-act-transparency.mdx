This post tries to sum up the essentials of the AI Act, and on top of that you get the downloadable icons the EU just released for labelling AI-generated content.

## 1. Risk classification

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 sets out four tiers. Which one applies doesn't depend on the tool you use, but on what you do with it and who it acts on.

## 2. What sets the risk tier

This is the point most often misread. Summarising internal minutes is very different from automatically filtering job applications. In the second case, you're making decisions that affect someone's access to employment.

And it applies to any organisation using AI — even AI contracted out to a third party. Knowing which platform you use isn't enough. You have to look at what it's for, who it acts on, and what the consequences are.

> Before classifying a system, ask yourself: what decision does it produce? About whom? Could it affect their rights or opportunities? Is anyone actually reviewing the output?

The icon has to meet four conditions:

- Be visible from the first moment
- Be built into the piece — not overlaid on top
- Stay visible when the content is shared or downloaded
- Come with plain-language text alongside it

**When you don't need to label:** when the content is evidently art, satire or fiction; when it's legally authorised for criminal investigation; or when the text has gone through human review with clear editorial responsibility. If a person signs off on it and reviews it, it isn't mandatory.

That said: using the icon alone doesn't guarantee compliance. The goal isn't to dodge fines. It's that nobody mistakes what's real for what's artificial.

## 4. AI Act implementation timeline

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| Feb 2025 | Prohibitions in force + basic AI literacy training |
| Aug 2025 | Rules for general-purpose models (GPT, Gemini…) |
| Aug 2026 | General application of the Regulation |
| Dec 2027 | High-risk rules for education, employment, biometrics and justice |
